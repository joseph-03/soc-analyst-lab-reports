------------------------------------------------------------
  1. SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------

This exercise examined how SOC Level 1 analysts document
security alerts, communicate investigation findings, and
escalate potentially serious threats to senior analysts.

The practical scenarios involved a sensitive document
shared externally and a suspicious email marked as
phishing after delivery. The exercise highlighted the
importance of structured reporting, evidence-based
analysis, and timely escalation.


------------------------------------------------------------
  2. PRACTICAL SCENARIO ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------

  Scenario 1 — Sensitive Document Shared Externally

  Indicators:
    - Alert identified as "Sensitive Document Share to External"
    - Source user and document-sharing activity required review
    - Potential exposure of sensitive organisational data

  Assessment:
    External sharing of a sensitive document may result
    in unauthorised disclosure. The user and activity
    details must be reviewed to understand the context
    and determine whether the sharing was authorised.

  Recommended Action:
    Document the source user, relevant activity, and
    available evidence. Escalate the alert if unauthorised
    disclosure or further investigation is suspected.


  Scenario 2 — Phishing Email Detected After Delivery

  Indicators:
    - Email flagged as phishing after delivery
    - Sender claimed to be Microsoft support
    - Email authentication checks showed failures
    - Urgency-based wording and an archive attachment
      raised additional concerns

  Assessment:
    The combination of failed email authentication checks
    and suspicious message characteristics suggests
    possible sender spoofing and a phishing attempt.

  Recommended Action:
    Prepare an alert report using the Five Ws:
    Who, What, When, Where, and Why. Include the sender,
    relevant email details, supporting indicators, and
    the reasoning behind the verdict. Escalate for
    further investigation when required.


------------------------------------------------------------
  3. KEY FINDINGS
------------------------------------------------------------

  - Alert reports preserve investigation context and
    help senior analysts continue the investigation.

  - The Five Ws provide a structured way to record
    relevant users, actions, timestamps, locations,
    and reasoning.

  - Failed email authentication checks can indicate
    spoofing when considered alongside other evidence.

  - Potential data exposure and phishing incidents
    require appropriate assessment and documentation.

  - Clear escalation and communication help ensure
    that serious alerts receive timely attention.


------------------------------------------------------------
  4. RECOMMENDED SECURITY CONTROLS
------------------------------------------------------------

  Preventive Controls:
    Apply appropriate data-sharing permissions, access
    restrictions, and email authentication protections.

  Detective Controls:
    Monitor external document sharing, suspicious email
    activity, and email authentication failures.

  Response Measures:
    Document evidence, record the alert verdict, escalate
    serious or unclear cases to the appropriate analyst,
    and coordinate with relevant teams when necessary.


------------------------------------------------------------
  5. CONCLUSION
------------------------------------------------------------

The exercise demonstrated that alert reporting is an
important part of SOC operations, extending beyond
identifying suspicious activity. Structured reports,
evidence-based reasoning, timely escalation, and clear
communication help senior analysts understand potential
threats and determine the appropriate response.
